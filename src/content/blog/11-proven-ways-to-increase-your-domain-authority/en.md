Two pages can target the same keyword, follow the same on-page best practices, and still land in very different positions on Google. One reason is domain authority — a measure of how much trust and ranking potential a site has earned over time.

According to Hero Blog, "A large number of backlinks indicates to Google that you're an authority to a large audience. The top Google result has 380% more backlinks than the results in positions two to ten."

This guide covers what domain authority is, how it's calculated, and — most importantly — eleven proven ways to increase it.

## What Is Domain Authority?

Domain Authority (DA) is a search engine ranking score developed by Moz to predict how well a website is likely to perform on search results pages. It's scored on a 1-to-100 scale: the higher the number, the stronger the site's ranking potential.

DA is driven primarily by the quality and quantity of a site's backlinks, and it correlates closely with search performance — sites with higher DA tend to rank higher, and vice versa. That said, DA is not itself a Google ranking factor. It's a third-party proxy for authority, not a metric Google consults directly.

The scale is logarithmic, which means gains get harder to come by as your score rises. Moving from a DA of 20 to 30 takes far less link equity than moving from 75 to 80 — worth keeping in mind when you set targets.

## Why Domain Authority Matters

Google weighs a site's authority and relevance heavily when determining where it ranks, and backlinks are central to both. Links from credible, high-performing sites signal that your content is trustworthy, which improves your odds of ranking well. Industry research consistently shows that most SEOs consider link building central to brand authority and visibility.

DA isn't a direct ranking input, but it's a solid proxy for overall site health. A rising score usually means your backlink profile — and by extension your ranking potential — is moving in the right direction.

## What Counts as a Good Domain Authority Score?

There's no universal "good," "bad," or "average" DA score. A site with a modest DA can still outrank stronger competitors if there's little competition for its target keywords, and a low score early on doesn't mean the site is low quality — it usually just means the backlink profile is still young.

Chasing a DA of 100 isn't a useful goal. You won't outscore Amazon or Apple, whose scores reflect more than a decade of accumulated high-quality links, and you don't need to. What matters is your DA relative to your actual competitors — sites targeting the same audience, topics, and keywords. If your backlink profile is stronger than theirs, your ranking potential is too.

## Why Did My Domain Authority Drop?

DA should trend upward as a site matures, but drops happen — and they're not always a red flag. Moz periodically updates its DA algorithm, and when it does, scores shift across the board, including your competitors'. Beyond algorithm updates, common causes of a falling DA include:

- Duplicate content flagged by Google
- A growing number of low-DA sites linking to yours
- Keyword stuffing
- A decline in overall content quality

## Domain Authority vs. Page Authority

Page Authority (PA) is a related Moz metric, also scored from 0 to 100 on a logarithmic scale, that predicts how a single page — rather than an entire domain — is likely to perform in search results.

The distinction is straightforward: DA reflects the overall ranking strength of a domain and its subdomains, while PA reflects the ranking strength of one specific page. A site can carry a strong DA while individual pages have weak PA, and vice versa.

## How Is Domain Authority Calculated?

Neither Moz nor Google has published the exact formula behind their respective authority metrics, and different tools weigh the underlying signals differently. What's publicly known is that Moz factors in inbound links, the quality of those links, the number of unique root domains linking to a site, content quality, and on-page SEO. Google, for context, is understood to use over 200 ranking signals — DA is not one of them.

Ahrefs uses a comparable but separate metric called Domain Rating (DR), also scored 0–100. DR is driven by three main factors:

- **The number of referring domains** — the sites linking to yours. Followed links count toward DR; nofollow, UGC, and sponsored links don't.
- **The authority of those referring domains** — a handful of links from strong domains does more for your DR than hundreds from weak ones. Consistent growth in the authority of your linking domains tends to lift your own DR over time.
- **How selectively those referring domains link out** — a domain that links to relatively few other sites passes more authority per link than one that links out indiscriminately.

DR is best read alongside other metrics — domain-level traffic or a page's individual URL Rating — rather than on its own; it measures link reputation, not overall site quality.

Checking your DA in Moz is straightforward: open the Link Explorer tool, enter your domain, and click "Check Authority." Once a month is a reasonable cadence, since DA moves gradually. Update frequency varies by tool: Moz's index refreshes close to real time, while Ahrefs indexes new links roughly every 15 minutes, though a link's full impact on your backlink profile can take up to two months to register.

## How to Increase Domain Authority: 11 Proven Tips

Improving domain authority is a gradual process — expect roughly 90 days before new backlinks are indexed and reflected in your score. There's no shortcut, but a consistent, well-targeted strategy compounds. Here's where to start.

## 1. Publish Link-Worthy Content

High-quality content is the foundation everything else is built on — mediocre content rarely earns links, no matter how much you promote it. According to Moz, blog posts over 3,000 words tend to attract more backlinks than shorter posts, largely because longer-form content goes deeper and delivers more value. Content built around original data or research is especially effective at earning links, since it gives other sites a genuine reason to cite you. Strong, well-optimized content also improves your organic visibility on its own, which in turn creates more opportunities for natural backlinks.

## 2. Conduct a Link Audit

A link audit — reviewing the backlinks pointing to your site to separate the good from the harmful — is worth running once your site is three to four months old, and periodically after that. Pull your backlink profile from Google Search Console, Ahrefs, Semrush, Moz, Majestic, or Bing Webmaster Tools, then:

- Check the spam score of each referring domain
- Compare your referring domains to your competitors'
- Track whether backlinks are growing steadily over time — a healthy sign
- Review your anchor text distribution
- Analyze at the page level first, rather than the domain as a whole

If most of your links come from high-DA domains, a handful of spammy ones won't meaningfully hurt you. But it's worth removing links that are trending toward hurting your score before they do.

As part of the audit, flag dead links — pages returning 403, 404, 410, or 503 errors — for cleanup. Also review your nofollow links: Google has confirmed it treats the nofollow attribute as a hint rather than a hard directive, meaning it may still pass ranking credit at its discretion. That makes nofollow links more valuable than they used to be, and less automatically dismissible during an audit.

## 3. Remove or Disavow Spammy Backlinks

A high concentration of spammy, low-DA backlinks is one of the most common causes of a declining domain rating. Start by asking the linking site's owner to remove the link directly — it rarely works, but it costs little to try.

If that fails, disavow the links:

- Compile a list of the spammy domains
- Submit them through Google's Disavow Links tool as a text file via Search Console
- Wait for Google to crawl and process the request — this can take time, especially for sites with many referring domains

## 4. Strengthen On-Page SEO

Strong content struggles to earn links from authoritative sites if the on-page fundamentals aren't in place — solid on-page SEO is what gets that content discovered and ranked in the first place. Tools like Semrush, Moz Site Crawl, and Screaming Frog can audit your pages for the essentials: H1 tags, keyword optimization, meta titles and descriptions, URL structure, keyword density, and image alt text. Fix what's missing or misconfigured before investing further in link building.

## 5. Run a Technical SEO Audit

Technical issues quietly undermine even strong content and backlink profiles. Moz's Site Crawl tool, among others, can surface problems like slow page speed, poor mobile usability, robots.txt misconfigurations, redirect errors, and canonical tag issues. Resolving these clears the way for the backlinks you've already earned — and the ones you're about to earn — to count for more.

## 6. Promote Your Content

Content that never reaches an audience won't earn links, regardless of quality. Distribute it across the marketing channels where your audience is active, with particular attention to social media, which tends to drive more engagement than most other channels. Traffic and visibility feed directly into authority. As social media analyst Ruby Rusine puts it: "Social signals impact a website's ranking and authority — be they likes, shares, views, or mentions."

## 7. Improve Your Internal Links

A thoughtful internal linking structure makes it easier for search engines to crawl and index your pages, while signaling the depth and organization of your content to both users and search engines. Well-structured internal links pass authority between pages and contribute, cumulatively, to your site's overall domain authority.

## 8. Reclaim Unlinked Brand Mentions

Your brand is likely mentioned across the web on sites that don't link back to you — these unlinked citations are backlink opportunities you're currently leaving on the table. To recover them:

- List the products, services, or terms associated with your brand
- Use a tool like Ahrefs' Content Explorer to find mentions of your brand name
- Filter results with Screaming Frog or BuzzStream to isolate mentions that aren't linked
- Reach out to the site owners and request that the mention be converted into a link

## 9. Build Relationships with Competitors and Peers

Sites covering similar topics to yours aren't only competitors — they're also potential collaborators. Identify bloggers and site owners publishing in your space with solid DA scores, and reach out with a specific, well-researched pitch. The best outreach is personal and value-driven rather than transactional; a generic ask is easy to ignore.

## 10. Fix Broken Links (Broken Link Building)

Site redesigns routinely leave a trail of dead pages and outdated URLs, and some of those broken pages likely still hold links pointing to your site. To reclaim that lost equity:

- Compile a list of your broken URLs (404 errors) using Ahrefs, Moz, or Majestic
- Use Screaming Frog or a similar crawler to confirm which pages return 404s
- Cross-check with Google Analytics to identify affected pages
- Replace the broken content with an updated, higher-value version
- Contact the linking site's owner and offer the current URL as a replacement — most site owners are glad to fix a broken link on their own page

This is consistently one of the higher-return link-building tactics, since you're offering a genuine fix rather than asking for a favor.

## 11. Write Guest Posts for Authoritative Sites

Guest posting remains one of the most reliable ways to earn backlinks and build topical authority. To do it well:

- Target sites in your niche with a DA of 50 or higher
- Research their audience and existing content before pitching
- Read their contributor guidelines closely
- Pitch an outline tailored to what their readers actually want

A well-placed guest post does double duty: it earns a quality backlink and positions you as a credible voice in your field. Two smaller-scale tactics worth testing alongside it are podcast guesting — appearing on niche podcasts that link back to guests — and leaving genuinely thoughtful comments on high-authority industry blogs, which can drive referral traffic even when the link-equity gain is modest.

Domain authority is a useful compass, not a destination. It won't guarantee a page-one ranking, and chasing the number for its own sake misses the point. Used well, it's a way to track whether your backlink profile and overall SEO strategy are moving in the right direction — and these eleven tactics, applied consistently, are how you move it.
