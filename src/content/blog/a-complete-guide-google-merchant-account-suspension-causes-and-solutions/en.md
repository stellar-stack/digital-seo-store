Waking up to a suspended Google Merchant Center account is unsettling, especially when Google doesn't explain why. If you sell products online, whether your store runs on Magento, Shopify, or any other platform, and Google has suspended your Merchant Center account, you're not alone. Suspensions are common, and in most cases recoverable, but there is no single fix that applies to every case, since Google isn't obligated to disclose the exact reason behind its decision. What you can do is understand how the process works and what typically triggers a suspension, so you can respond on solid ground rather than guesswork. This guide walks through how suspensions unfold, the most common causes we see across accounts, and how to respond effectively when it happens to you.

## How You'll Know Your Account Has Been Suspended

Before you receive a suspension notice, your account typically moves through three stages.

**Initial review.** When you upload products to Google Merchant Center, each item goes through an initial review that can take up to three business days. During this window, items sit in "pending" status while Google checks them against its Shopping Ads policies. If an item doesn't comply, Google treats it as suspicious and can suspend the account even at this early stage. If everything passes, your products become eligible to appear in the Google Shopping experience.

**Warning period.** Google also runs ongoing checks on live listings to confirm they continue meeting policy requirements. When it finds a mismatch or missing information, Google typically sends a warning email describing the issue and how to fix it, rather than suspending the account outright. During this period, your products keep running in Shopping ads, and the Diagnostics tab in Merchant Center flags the issue, giving you a window to correct it before further action is taken.

**Account suspension.** If the issue isn't resolved in time, Google suspends the account: your products stop appearing in Shopping ads and other sponsored placements. Sometimes the notice states exactly which policy was violated; often it doesn't. Google typically indicates it will re-review your account and website before deciding whether to lift the suspension, which means there's usually still an opportunity to fix the underlying issue and request reinstatement.

Once you understand these three stages, the next step is identifying what actually caused the suspension.

## Why Google Suspends Merchant Center Accounts

No two businesses run into exactly the same issue, and the right cause is often industry-specific. That said, a relatively small set of issues accounts for the large majority of suspensions.

- **Inexact shipping costs.** If the shipping cost listed in Merchant Center doesn't match what customers see on your website at checkout, Google will flag the discrepancy. Make sure the shipping costs in your feed mirror your site exactly.
- **Misrepresentation of products or services.** Shopping ads that mislead users, intentionally or not, are treated as untrustworthy. This includes ads that don't accurately represent the product, that omit information needed for an informed purchase decision, that fail to disclose a refund or return policy, or that don't make the total price clear before checkout.
- **Inaccurate prices, availability, or condition.** Google checks listed prices, stock availability, and product condition against what actually appears on your site. Mismatches in any of these are treated as policy violations.
- **Incomplete payment details.** Missing or unclear payment information creates a poor user experience and can also read as a signal of an unauthorized or fraudulent sale. Make sure your checkout clearly presents all necessary payment information.
- **Delivery issues.** If you can't reliably fulfill and ship orders to the countries you target, that's a policy violation. Confirm your delivery capabilities match what you advertise.
- **Insufficient or incorrect contact details.** Your physical address, email, and phone number need to appear in your site footer and match your Contact Us page exactly. Any inconsistency here is an easy flag for Google's review systems.
- **Selling restricted or dangerous products.** Google prohibits items such as weapons, explosives, drugs, and related paraphernalia. Review Google's policy on dangerous products before listing anything in a regulated category.
- **Broken or unreachable landing pages.** Every shopping ad must lead to a working page that reflects the product being advertised. A 404 error, an "under construction" page, or any broken URL will trigger a suspension.
- **Affiliate or click spam.** Sending users somewhere other than what the ad or call-to-action promises, for example, a "Buy Now" button that lands on your homepage instead of the product page, is treated as deceptive routing.
- **A risky checkout experience.** Your checkout needs a valid SSL certificate, a way to navigate back without losing progress, and a standard, recognizable purchase flow. Anything that feels insecure or unfamiliar to shoppers can be read by Google as a checkout risk.
- **Inappropriate images.** Product images containing placeholder content, promotional overlays ("free shipping," "buy now"), watermarks, or merchant logos and branding are not allowed in the product feed.
- **Missing or incomplete legal pages.** Your site needs clearly accessible terms and conditions, a privacy policy, and return/refund and shipping policies, written in the language and for the region you're targeting. If customers can't find or read these, it counts against you.
- **Restricted business categories.** Some categories carry outright restrictions, including alcohol, firearms, adult services, and gambling. Others draw closer scrutiny because of a history of fraud in the category, such as unauthorized resellers, weight-loss products, financial services, third-party technical support, addiction treatment, loan services, and cryptocurrency-related services. Operating in any of these spaces increases the likelihood of review and suspension.
- **Circumventing the system.** Attempting to game Google's review process, whether through cloaking, misleading submissions, or other workarounds, is one of the most serious triggers for suspension, and the hardest to reverse. The only reliable way to avoid it is to keep your feed and site information accurate and consistent at all times.

## What Not to Do When Your Account Is Suspended

- Don't panic and file an appeal before you understand the issue.
- Don't assume the suspension is a mistake, or that you haven't violated any policy, without checking first.
- Don't attempt to create a new Merchant Center or Google Ads account to work around the suspension.
- Don't let frustration show in a written appeal or in conversations with Google's support team.
- Don't submit multiple, repeated appeals in quick succession. Each one resets the review process and can make Google more cautious, not less.

## What to Do When Your Account Is Suspended

- Read every email and notification from Google carefully. It's often the clearest signal you'll get about what triggered the suspension.
- Check whether the specific issue Google flagged is visible on your site or in your feed.
- Audit your website and Merchant Center feed in full. Look beyond the flagged issue: suspensions are sometimes triggered by more than one policy gap at once.
- Decide whether to handle the appeal yourself or work with a partner experienced in Merchant Center reinstatements, who can help you build a stronger, more precise case.
- Submit a clear, factual written appeal that explains what you believe caused the suspension and exactly what you've changed to fix it. Vague or defensive appeals are far less likely to succeed.
